Campus licensing

uqulang for universities

uqulang is licensed to universities and research institutions. One campus licence covers every student, every member of staff and every machine in your labs, with deployment that works on an air-gapped network.

Who it is for

Systems courses

Operating systems, compilers, computer architecture, embedded — any course where the point is what the machine actually does.

Introductory programming

A small, explicit language with diagnostics written to be read by somebody who is still learning to read them.

Research groups

Reproducible native builds with a pinned toolchain, so a result from 2026 still rebuilds bit-for-bit in 2029.

What a campus licence includes

IncludedDetail
Unlimited seats Every enrolled student and every member of teaching and research staff, on campus machines and their own.
Lab deployment Image-ready archives for macOS, Linux and Windows, with offline activation so lab machines never contact a licence server.
Course starter kit Lab exercises with worked solutions, sample assignments, and machine-readable test output (uqu test --format json) for autograders.
Version pinning A named release held stable for the length of a semester, so a mid-term upgrade never breaks a running course.
Faculty onboarding A training session for the teaching team before the first semester, plus written migration notes for every release.
Support Named technical contacts with the response targets below.

A reduced test case is the single most useful thing you can attach to a report, followed by the output of uqu doctor.

Evaluating it first

Teaching staff can request a time-limited evaluation key covering a single course or a pilot lab, with the full toolchain and the starter kit. Nothing about the evaluation build is cut down — it is the shipping compiler.

  • Run a pilot for one semester before committing to a campus licence.
  • Keep the material you write during the pilot; it stays yours.
  • Ask for a syllabus review — we will say when uqulang is the wrong fit.

Roadmap

Dates are intentions, not commitments. The order is more stable than the timing, and licensed institutions are told before anything on it changes.

Asking for changes

Licensed institutions shape the language. Feature requests go through your named contact, are answered in writing, and the accepted ones appear on the roadmap above with the release they are scheduled for. Requests that come from a course that is actually running carry the most weight — teaching friction is the most useful signal we get.

Using the name and mark

The name “uqulang” and the mark may be used in course descriptions, syllabi, lecture material, academic papers and talks, and to say that software was written in uqulang. They may not be used as the name of another product, or in a way that implies a partnership that does not exist.
